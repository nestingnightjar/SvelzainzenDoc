THE MOST COMFORTABLE MOLD
“Believe me when I tell you: it’s the most comfy thing in the world.”

(S1)1-13-1, commonly described as a mold, grows several common features in irregular patterns. Such features include pustules, orifices, and roots. Roots are the growth feature of the Svelzainzen, spreading thin, finger-like growths outwards in search of desirable environments for the development of postules. Postules are the primary mass of the Svelzainzen, being large, rotund growths covered in a thin, hairlike filament. It is common to see thorn-like barbs running atop the larger postules. Orifices develop the same way that postules do. Orifices resemble hollow pustules, their interiors covered in a much denser layer of filament alongside a mucus-like secretion. Hypnotized employees will seek orifices out to enter them, after which the structure closes and seals the employee inside for digestion.
